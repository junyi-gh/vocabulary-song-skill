# Style, arrangement, and voice presets

Use these as starting points and adapt them to the lyric's subject. A generation prompt should describe the musical journey, not merely list genre tags.

## Pop

- Instruments: piano or clean guitar, bass, acoustic/electronic drums, synth texture.
- Arc: restrained verse, lifting pre-chorus, memorable full chorus, reduced bridge, final chorus with harmony.
- Learning fit: clear hooks and repetition; keep production from masking consonants.
- Voices: clear mezzo-soprano, light tenor, or balanced duet.

## Acoustic folk

- Instruments: acoustic guitar, upright or warm electric bass, brushed percussion, mandolin/violin color.
- Arc: intimate storytelling that gradually adds harmony and strings.
- Learning fit: excellent diction and narrative context; avoid overly loose rubato.
- Voices: warm alto, baritone, or close two-part harmony.

## Rock

- Instruments: electric guitars, bass, live drums, optional organ or piano.
- Arc: tense verse, wide chorus, dynamic bridge and final lift.
- Learning fit: strong stress and energy; keep distortion below the vocal.
- Voices: bright powerful tenor, gritty alto, or call-and-response duet.

## R&B / soul

- Instruments: electric piano, bass, pocket drums, clean guitar, restrained strings or organ.
- Arc: intimate verse, emotionally expanding chorus, expressive bridge, layered final harmonies.
- Learning fit: strong emotion and natural phrasing; avoid melisma on target words.
- Voices: warm alto, smooth tenor, or soulful duet with crisp consonants.

## Jazz

- Instruments: piano, upright bass, brushed drums, saxophone or muted trumpet.
- Arc: conversational verse, elegant refrain, short instrumental response, final vocal return.
- Learning fit: slower tempos help articulation; avoid reharmonization that obscures the hook.
- Voices: smoky alto, relaxed baritone, or intimate duet.

## Electronic

- Instruments: synth bass, programmed drums, pads, arpeggiator or pluck lead.
- Arc: sparse opening, build, melodic drop with sung hook, atmospheric bridge.
- Learning fit: rhythmic repetition; leave spectral room around the lead vocal.
- Voices: airy soprano, clean tenor, vocoder harmony only as a secondary texture.

## Hip-hop

- Instruments: drums, bass, keys or sample-like harmonic texture, melodic hook instrument.
- Arc: narrative verses, fully sung chorus, breakdown or spoken bridge.
- Learning fit: excellent for syllable rhythm; target words must also appear in the sung hook or standard-pronunciation track.
- Voices: articulate rapper plus sung lead, or one versatile lead with layered hook.

## Cinematic

- Instruments: piano, strings, low percussion, bass, optional guitar or restrained synth.
- Arc: small human opening, gradual orchestral expansion, emotional climax, reflective ending.
- Learning fit: creates depth for difficult random sets; keep orchestration under the consonants.
- Voices: intimate alto, expressive tenor, duet, or choir used only after clear solo introductions.

## Prompt checklist

Include genre, mood, BPM, key, meter, lead vocal profile, supporting vocals, instrument roles, energy arc, diction requirement, target-word treatment, and unwanted traits. Useful exclusions include heavy reverb, buried vocals, excessive melisma, long instrumental intros, and artist imitation.
